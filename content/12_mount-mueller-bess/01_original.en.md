**1. Executive Summary:**

- **Purpose:** A pre-lodgement consultation for the Mount Mueller Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) (400 MW, 4-hour) and its associated overhead transmission lines near Gladstone. The meeting aimed to clarify approval pathways, key constraints, application strategy, and specific technical requirements from both the local Council and the State Assessment and Referral Agency (SARA).

- **Site/Project Overview:** The BESS is proposed for elevated private land at 525 Reed Road, west of Gladstone, connecting to Powerlink’s Calipiri Terminal Station. Two indicative transmission line routes are being considered, both traversing vegetated and marine environments.

- **Key Constraints & Risks:** The highest risks identified are in terrestrial ecology (Threatened Ecological Communities - TECs, regulated vegetation) and coastal/marine environments (marine plants, potential for threatened species like the water mouse). Bushfire risk is significant due to surrounding vegetation, while flooding is a moderate concern, as the BESS site itself is elevated.

- **Approvals & Process:**

  - **Transmission Line:** Both Council and SARA indicated the transmission line is unlikely to be considered ancillary to the BESS due to its distinct and higher impacts. It will likely require a separate application, with the assessment manager depending on the final alignment. A ministerial determination to consolidate the applications is a potential pathway.

  - **Lodgement Strategy:** There is a preference to lodge operational works applications (e.g., vegetation clearing, marine plants) concurrently with the Material Change of Use (MCU) application to streamline assessment, especially where impacts are well-defined.

  - **Key Submissions:** A Relevant Purpose Determination (RPD) under the Waste Management Act must be finalized before the MCU is submitted. An EPBC referral will proceed as needed. A comprehensive decommissioning security framework is required at lodgement, along with digital spatial data.

- **Technical Studies & Consultation:**

  - **Fire & Risk:** A preliminary fire safety study is acceptable at lodgement, with a detailed study to be conditioned. Early and documented engagement with QFES (both Bushfire Resilient Communities and Critical Infrastructure teams) is required for guidance on firefighting water supply and containment.

  - **Flood:** The design flood level must be 0.2% AEP, factoring in climate change.

  - **Ecology & Water:** Siting has focused on avoiding TECs. Detailed assessments for marine plants, water quality (including MUSIC modeling for the GBR catchment), geotechnical conditions, and acid sulfate soils are required at lodgement.

  - **Other:** Assessments for visual impact, agricultural land, and aviation impacts are necessary, along with a comprehensive stakeholder engagement register (including Powerlink, DTMR, Council, Queensland Rail, etc.).

- **Next Steps:** The proponent (GCGF/Fife) will confirm the transmission line route and secure landowner consents. SARA will issue written advice incorporating feedback from technical agencies (TCS, DPI, MSQ). A second pre-lodgement meeting focused on the transmission line will be scheduled once the route is confirmed.

### Consultation Q&A

### Background

Pre-lodgement consultation for the proposed Mount Mueller Battery Storage Facility (BESS) and associated overhead transmission lines at 525 Reed Road, west of Gladstone, developed by Green Thinker Green Future (GCGF). The BESS is proposed at 400 MW with four-hour duration. Participants included GCGF, environmental/planning consultants (Five NGH Five, CED Consulting), and the State Assessment and Referral Agency (SARA), with broader engagement noted with Queensland Fire and Emergency Services (QFES/QFD), local councils (Gladstone Regional Council and a Western Regional Council), and technical agencies. The meeting covered project overview, site constraints, approval pathways, ancillary status of transmission infrastructure, coastal/marine triggers, bushfire and flood standards, geotechnical and water quality requirements (Great Barrier Reef Marine Catchment), visual impact, stakeholder consultation, planning scheme assessments, decommissioning security framework, lodgement data expectations, and timelines for written advice.

### Q&A

##### 1. Transmission line ancillary status and reconfiguration

- SARA applies two tests to determine ancillary status: (1) clear functional relationship to the BESS and (2) subordination in scale and impact.

- Preliminary view: the transmission line is unlikely to be ancillary due to crossings of marine environments and highly vegetated areas, with impacts substantially different from the battery’s.

- Reconfiguration to achieve ancillary status is unlikely where both substation connection options cross a marine environment.

- For long-term leases or access easements, these would not be ancillary. A combined application (battery plus reconfiguring a lot) may be pursued via ministerial request for a single assessment manager as “battery and other assessable development.”

- Assessment manager and pathway depend on alignment/location (e.g., SDA, strategic coal land, Western Regional Council), with a ministerial determination potentially consolidating components.

##### 2. Operational works and permits at lodgement

- No strict requirement to lodge operational works (e.g., vegetation clearing) upfront with the MCU; strategy-dependent.

- Preference for lodging vegetation clearing permits concurrently with the battery application.

- For this site, marine plant impact permits will likely be known at MCU stage; SARA recommends lodging them together with sufficient detail.

##### 3. EPBC referral, RPD, and environmental offsets

- RPD to be finalized prior to MCU submission; EPBC referral handled prior to MCU if required.

- Environmental offsets are addressed post-approval. If significant residual impacts on MSES occur, approvals will include offset conditions; type (financial or performance-based) is chosen by the proponent. State and federal offset conditions may be concurrent and aligned.

##### 4. Fire safety and risk assessment

- Preliminary fire safety study at lodgement is acceptable; a final detailed study will be conditioned later once technology is confirmed. SARA can provide written advice on final plan content.

- A desktop Risk Management Assessment Report is acceptable at lodgement; onsite investigation may follow if desktop findings warrant it.

##### 5. Firefighting water supply guidance and QFES/QFD engagement

- No fixed SARA guidelines for firefighting water supply/containment quantities; depend on site design and number of battery units.

- Engage QFES/QFD before lodgement; QFES/QFD serve as third-party advisors. Document this engagement in the application.

- Batteries likely fall under “essential community infrastructure.” No specific BAL number; adopt a conservative approach and consult QFES/QFD.

- Expect engagement with QFD’s bushfire resilient communities team and critical infrastructure team; address QFD’s “Ten EIS recommendations” within State Code 27 reports.

##### 6. Flood design standards and initial modelling feedback

- SARA expects design to the 0.2% AEP level with climate change considerations.

- SARA can review initial flood modelling, stormwater master plans, and summaries; this is standard practice.

##### 7. Coastal Management District (CMD) and tidal considerations

- Technical questions on infrastructure above HAT within the coastal zone and operational works triggers are referred to TCS, DPI, and MSQ; written advice to follow.

##### 8. Follow-up meeting on transmission line coastal/marine matters

- SARA is open to another meeting with specialists once transmission line details and positions are finalized.

##### 9. Geotechnical, acid sulfate soils, and water quality

- Provide geotechnical and acid sulfate soils assessments at lodgement where possible.

- Emphasis on water quality due to Great Barrier Reef Marine Catchment; document water quality targets and stormwater volume reduction (SVP) requirements in the stormwater management plan.

- MUSIC-based pre- and post-development water quality and hydrology modelling expected; consider Great Barrier Reef Marine Management objectives.

##### 10. Visual impact assessment triggers

- Triggers are case-by-case, guided by local planning scheme overlays and site context (sensitive land uses).

- Assess sensitive receivers, proximity, impacts, mitigation; consider regionally significant features (e.g., Kaliopi River) and document.

##### 11. Stakeholder consultation benchmarks

- Project-specific engagement with DTMR, Queensland Rail, Council, Western Ports Corporation; document in Traffic Report and over-mass strategy.

- Identify transport pinch points and mitigation; maintain a stakeholder register. Social impact consultations may extend at detailed design.

##### 12. Planning scheme assessment expectations

- Provide a high-level assessment for a VSF application with tailored detailed responses to relevant overlay codes (e.g., airport environs).

- If SARA becomes assessment manager for components (e.g., transmission line), prepare a comprehensive assessment against the planning scheme (design, overlay, and use codes).

##### 13. Powerlink connection process documentation

- Document the current stage of the connection process and access to connection assets to demonstrate compliance with State Code 27.

##### 14. Airport corporation consultation and aviation impact

- Existing engagement via technical consultant with Gladstone Airport Corporation is acceptable if documented; SARA will undertake its own engagement upon submission and may provide further advice.

##### 15. Agricultural land assessment

- Where no agricultural land is mapped over the footprint, a technical memorandum/high-level statement by a suitably qualified person demonstrating compliance with Schedule 27 POs is acceptable; can be a head-back report.

##### 16. Decommissioning security framework

- Provide a decommissioning security framework at lodgement outlining financial instruments and security across all project stages, addressing scenarios such as liquidation and fire damage. Guidance available on SARA’s website; security must be maintained throughout the project life.

##### 17. Relevant purpose determination and digital data

- Lodge digital data with the application to allow SARA to verify alignment with the relevant purpose determination; misalignment can render development prohibited.

##### 18. EAR status and next steps

- EAR is mostly complete, pending updates to the footprint.

- SARA awaits technical agencies’ advice on coastal/marine questions and will provide written advice in the next week or so; open to a follow-up meeting on transmission line, ancillary tests, and coastal/marine matters.
